## Sentences to correct
1. **"Every financed result is fragile".** This wording appears on the live findings page (`site/round2.js`, lines 183 and 192), in `dev/reports/s64-findings-page-text.md` (lines 40 and 54), in `s64-round2-overview.md` (line 22), in `s64-round2-q2-robustness.md` (point 4) and in the v5.8.0 release notes in `CONTRIBUTING.md`. The data contradict it. The release design's basic-living effect, paid for by a contribution, is robust in Reference at both horizons: 116 of 116 readings keep the sign with an interval excluding zero, and none reverses (−5.1 and −5.8 points; `dev/runs/s64-round2/s64-q2-curves.md`, financed table). The correct statement is: "46 of 48 financed poverty and wealth curves are fragile; the two that hold are the release design's basic-living effect in Reference." The financed rows stay as D23 sets them, shown and never led with.
2. **"15 to 53 points a year without backing".** This appears in `s64-round2-overview.md` (line 36) and in the findings-page text's list of unfavourable results. At no backing the range is **18.2 to 53.3** points a year. The figure "15 to 53" (14.9 to 53.3) is the range at the model's own backing mechanism, taken over both readings (with and without its ceiling), and the text uses it correctly in that place. The live page avoids the number ("tens of points a year").
3. **"C2.2 does about one and a half to five times as much as the release design".** This is the one-sentence answer in the findings-page text; it is not on the live page. Over the 24 poverty and wealth effects the ratio runs from **1.4 to 7.1**. It is 1.4 to 3.4 in Reference and Adverse and 4.7 to 7.1 in Stress, and median wealth has the highest ratio in every cell.
4. **"26.0 to 33.3 points".** This is C2.2's basket-poverty range in the findings-page text. The lowest reading is 25.945, so the range is **25.9** to 33.3. The page itself prints from the data.
5. **Where the labour-force split is.** The live page's "Who gains" paragraph says the file r2-distribution holds the split of basket poverty by labour-force reason. It does not. The split is in the question 6 report and in `r2-erosion-104691f/tables.md`. The pointer should name those, or the split should be added to the data file.

Two smaller wording points:
- The question 2 report says the financed curves have 117 points, but its tables count 116 (115 for the year debt passes GDP). The report explains why the Source rows have 61 points instead of 62, but gives no such reason for the financed rows.
- "Labour's share falls by 10 to 30 points" holds for Reference at 20 years (11.7 and 30.5). Across every cell the fall is 1.1 to 32.1 points. A Hub sentence should name the cell it describes.

## For S40 (the Hub)
- **Take every number from the data files (`r2-*.json` at tag v5.8.0), not from the reports' prose.** Points 2 to 4 above are errors in the prose only.
- **`optimal-transfer-design.html`.** The v5.6.0 passage is correct for release 1, which says the release design leaves more adults with under $25,000 of wealth (44.4% against 32.7%). Release 2 shows the opposite. Under full backing, the design's own statement, the release design lowers wealth poverty by 10.9 points (yearly average, Reference, 20 years). The two releases also measure different things. Release 1 takes the share at the end of the run, with all wealth held as cash. Release 2 takes a yearly average, with wealth held by asset class. The no-programme levels therefore differ too (basket poverty 49.3% in release 1 against 36.1% in release 2). The page needs release labels and the backing statement beside the wealth figure. One release's figures should never be set against the other's.
- **Inflation sentences** (`dual-currency-inflation.html` "65% lower than UBI", `cco-ptf-integrated-framework.html` "contain local inflation to 3.2%", and others). These are already marked to correct or remove in `data/claims.csv`. Release 2 supports "no material inflation" only under full backing, which is a design statement. The model's own mechanism gives 15 to 53 points a year. Under preregistration rule 4, every inflation figure carries that statement beside it.

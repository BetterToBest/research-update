**Question 3:** "Head count, not depth" - only explanation? Alternative explanations.
- The pattern: FGT0 (headcount) worsens (W>0), FGT2 (severity) improves (W2<0) for the take-part group.
- Alternatives:
  1. **Ceiling/Floor effects**: The poverty line is a floor. If people are pushed from just above to just below, headcount rises, severity (squared gap) might not rise as much if they are close to line. But the report says severity *rises less with programme* (W2 negative). In no-programme, they might be far below (deep poverty). With programme, they are held just above, then fall just below. So depth is shallower. This is the "head count not depth" story.
  2. **Selection into opt-in group**: The "take-part" latent group might be systematically different (e.g., higher earners, closer to poverty line). Displacement hits them harder relatively. But the latent groups are fixed.
  3. **Price effects / Programme inflation**: The programme causes inflation, which erodes real income for everyone, but the payout protects participants. Non-participants bear inflation without payout. This could push participants closer to line. But the trace shows W_Non also positive (non-participants worsen too).
  4. **Baseline poverty rate lower with programme**: "the baseline poverty rate being lower with the programme so there is more room to rise". The programme cuts poverty (headcount) at D=0. So at D>0, the *potential* for increase is larger in programme run because starting point is lower. This is a mechanical "regression to the mean" or "floor effect" at zero poverty. If programme eliminates deep poverty, then displacement can only create shallow poverty. This would show as headcount rise but severity rise muted. This is a strong alternative: the programme compresses the distribution at the bottom, so any shock increases headcount but not depth.
  5. **Measurement**: FGT2 weights by square of gap. If gaps are small (just below line), FGT2 small. If programme truncates large gaps, FGT2 rises less.
- Extra measurement to tell apart: The distribution of distances to the poverty line (or density near the line) in both runs at D=0 and D>0. Or the FGT1 (poverty gap index) which is linear in gap. If FGT1 rises proportionally to FGT0, it's headcount. If FGT1 rises less, it's shallow. The trace has FGT0 and FGT2. FGT1 would help. Also, the adult-level distance to line at D=0 for the take-part group vs non-participants. The report says "The stored values do not show the adults just above the line". So that measurement is missing.

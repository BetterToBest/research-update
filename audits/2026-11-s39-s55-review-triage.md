## In plain words

- **The reviews agree with each other and with the project's own reading on the big things.** The price rule (all unmatched new money reaches prices) decides the inflation and wealth results; the outside model (BeforeIT) could not test it because its prices cannot answer demand; no study covers new money of 30-50% of household income a year; a falling wealth Gini beside an 80% fall in median real wealth must not be presented as "less inequality"; the S55 wealth gain depends on PTF growth toward the 49% ceiling at the design's at-cost prices.
- **Three real errors in published text were found, all wording, no figure:** (1) the S39 report said the check "marks out the range ... 0 to 1 in pass-through", which reads as a bound on the real value; it is only the range between the two engines' assumptions (dated correction note in the S39 report; also in the audit prompt and the round-2 note). (2) The study preregistration labelled the published wealth Gini "with debts kept"; it counts debts as zero (Amendment 12). (3) MODEL_SPEC gave only the release row's interval formula, while the study reports use paired-bootstrap intervals (note added).
- **Two review claims were wrong.** Both say the headline runs on the model's too-low wages; the headline has used US (CPS ASEC) earnings since the study programme was preregistered (Oct 30), and the 49.2% → 27.1% shift they cite is that switch. ChatGPT reports an unexplained gap between 21.9% and 33.4% of families in debt; the two figures are different engine settings (explained in item D1).
- **Most cited sources check out.** Five could not be found or say something different: Grok's San Francisco Fed "0.4-0.7 points per 10% of GDP", Grok's grocery margin of 2.1% (FMI's own release gives 1.7% for 2025), ChatGPT's "19% spent more than their income" (not in the 2025 SHED), ChatGPT's QJE publication of the guaranteed-income study (found only as an NBER paper, with different figures), and ChatGPT's "beta-Pareto fits the Forbes 400 better" (the paper found says other distributions also fit adequately). These are not used.
- **What changes in the plan:** S57-S65 gain the reviews' measures, sources, parameter ranges and tests (PLAN.md); S60 now waits on S61 (a worker's outside option needs the non-working adults first); a new step **S66** traces why wealth is better at a 10% fixed PTF share than at 20-30% before anyone leans on that curve; S7's findings page gets the reviews' wording rules. Two points would change the design, not the model of it, and go to Duke as ledger decisions **D19** (who pays when PTF prices do not cover running costs) and **D20** (the 40% and 55% tests as hard steps or a smooth curve). Each has a default, so nothing waits.

## A. S55's design and statistics

**A1. The density tests read the PTFs' share of essential sales (G-A1, C-A1). Adapt.**
Both say sales share is a defensible proxy for market penetration but not a literal reading of "merchant participation", and that membership (21.6%) would never pass the 40% test. The sales-share reading is Duke's confirmed reading of the design (D16, Nov 7), so it stays the main reading. Round 2 adds labelled readings beside it: the tests read members' share of adults, and the share of demand the PTFs' capacity can serve (S62 builds, S63 preregisters). ChatGPT's further step, replacing the hard 40% and 55% gates by a continuous relationship until evidence supports a threshold, changes the design: **ledger D20** (default: keep the papers' tests in the main row and add a smooth-curve reading beside it). S55's own finding (no step at 55%, a small one at 40% in 2 of 180 cases) is reported as it stands.

**A2. The 49% demand ceiling (G-A2, C-A2). Accept.**
S Group's 49.0% of Finnish grocery sales in 2025 is confirmed (PTY, the Finnish Grocery Trade Association). The comparators are real but have different denominators and market structures: US public power about 15% and cooperatives about 13% of electricity customers (APPA; year not stated on the page); Colorado Option 47% of 2025 selections on its state exchange (Colorado Division of Insurance); Washington's public option about 30% of marketplace buyers in 2025 (an Exchange official quoted by Stateline, Mar 2, 2026; no official report found); Nevada's Battle Born plans about 10% of 2026 marketplace enrolment (Nevada Health Link figures, about 10.3% in state minutes); US food co-ops: 169 co-ops, 241 stores, about $2.8 billion a year (NCG), a tiny national share. They are not pooled into one parameter. The 49% stays a labelled design parameter; 75% and 100% are called stress tests, not forecasts (S7, S64). Grok's "S Group charges market prices and remains profitable" has no source given and was not verified; it is not used.

**A3. The operations limit cannot bind because the design prices at cost (G-A3, C-A3). Accept the readings; the gap goes to Duke.**
Both: not-for-profit status removes outside dividends, not the need to cover wages, occupancy, losses, depreciation, debt service and reserves. S55 already shows the stakes (at outside sector margins sales cover 35-59% of running costs and the network never grows). S62 builds three readings: the current at-cost design; full-cost break-even (prices cover operating and capital-replacement costs); and observed sector costs and margins. PTF capacity is also limited by staff, not only capital. Which reading joins the main row is S63's ledger decision, filed before any result. Who pays if PTF prices sit below full cost (prices rise; an explicit subsidy from the Source counted as programme cost; or the Hub's lower running costs taken as stated) is the design: **ledger D19**. Grok's "default to outside costs now" is declined until then.
Sources: Census Annual Integrated Economic Survey 2024 (food and beverage stores, NAICS 445: about $982.2 billion revenue, about 3.40 million employees; confirmed); CRS R48252 (2024): child care "profits of less than 1%" for most providers, personnel "often the largest share" of a centre's budget (confirmed); FMI's own release puts 2025 food-retail net margins at 1.7% (Grok's 2.1% appears only in a secondary briefing; not used); NGA/FMS independent grocers 1.9% net in fiscal 2024 (trade press summary; S62 opens the study). EIA Form 861, NSECE and Child Care Aware are datasets S62 opens.

## B. S39 and the price rule

**B1. Was BeforeIT a meaningful cross-check? (G-B1, C-B1). Accept.**
Both: it shows a published model can give zero by construction; it does not validate or refute the engine, and it does not bound the real pass-through. Correction note added. Better comparisons named: a multisector model with binding capacity constraints (Comin, Johnson and Jones, "Supply Chain Constraints and Inflation", NBER w31179 / FEDS 2023-075: binding constraints explain about half of the 2021-22 inflation rise; confirmed), a structural VAR of US fiscal transfers (Arias, Rubio-Ramírez and Shin, Philadelphia Fed WP 26-23, May 2026; confirmed, B2), and local transfer experiments (B2). They inform S58's rule and its checks. Declined for round 2: a US-calibrated BeforeIT with non-uniform starting prices (S39 section 7: calibration does not give its firms a response to excess demand), and HANK or FRB/US-style models (too large for this round; noted as literature). Grok's EURACE and CATS were named without sources and not opened.

**B2. Evidence on how much large transfers raise prices (G-B2, C-B2). Accept.**
Both conclude, and the sources confirm, that **no study covers new money of 30-50% of household income every year**. Opened:
- Egger, Haushofer, Miguel, Niehaus and Walker, *Econometrica* 2022 (Kenya; one-time transfers over 15% of local GDP): average price inflation 0.1%, upper 95% bound 0.22%, under 1% at the peak; the authors attribute it to slack capacity. Confirmed in the paper's text.
- Cunha, De Giorgi and Jayachandran, *Review of Economic Studies* 2019 (Mexico): cash raised prices about 0.2%, not significant; larger in remote villages. Confirmed (abstract, J-PAL summary). The paper is "The Price Effects of Cash Versus In-Kind Transfers" (2019; ChatGPT dates it 2018).
- Filmer, Friedman, Kandpal and Onishi, *Review of Economics and Statistics* 2023 (Philippines): prices of perishable protein rose in some markets, more in remote and saturated ones. Confirmed.
- Allen and Gentilini, IFPRI brief, Oct 2025: minimal average price effects for most goods, larger where supply is inelastic. Confirmed.
- de Soyres, Santacreu and Young (FEDS Note, Jul 2022: about 2.5 points of US inflation from pandemic fiscal support; St. Louis Fed WP 2022-019: about 2.6 points). Both reviews' figures are confirmed; they are two versions of one estimate.
- Arias, Rubio-Ramírez and Shin, Philadelphia Fed WP 26-23: pandemic measures about 11% of 2019 GDP; fiscal transfer shocks raised the GDP deflator "roughly five percent by the end of 2021". Confirmed in the paper.
- Grok's San Francisco Fed "0.4-0.7 points per 10% of GDP, larger when sentiment is high": **not found**. The SF Fed letter found (Economic Letter 2022-07) attributes about 3 points of US inflation through 2021 to fiscal support. Not used.
These are order-of-magnitude checks for S58, never targets (research rule 3).

**B3. A price rule that answers demand and capacity (G-B3, C-B3). Adapt.**
Grok proposes a markup over cost times (1 + φ × excess demand^γ); ChatGPT a sector rule, change in log price = α × cost change + ρ × expected inflation + κ × max(0, utilisation − u*), with output, inventories or rationing answering first where there is slack. S58 builds the second form (it can produce pass-through above 1 through expectations, which the first cannot) for the engine's essentials (food, housing, medical) and a general basket, behind `PRICE_RULE`. Every parameter is sourced or labelled and swept; Grok's φ in 0.2-2 and γ in 1-2 have no source and are taken only as a first sweep range. BeforeIT's 1.5-times output stretch is that model's assumption, labelled if used. Not checked here: whether the engine's general economy has a capacity measure (Grok says "K is already in the engine"; the engine has PTF capacity); S58 checks first. Tests from both reviews go into S58 (no new money → today's path; slack → output rises, prices little; at capacity → prices or rationing rise; more capacity → less pressure; a supply shock moves prices with no new money; tax-financed differs from money-financed).

**B4. The conversion tax (G-B4, C-B4). Accept.**
Net it only to the extent it leaves spendable circulation; if the Source spends it later, that spending enters in the year it happens. S58 builds a consolidated money-flow table (BU issued, conversion paid, tax retained, tax re-spent, Source outlays, matched output, price subsidies, balances held) and defines the net injection from it. What the Source does with the tax is first read from the Hub papers (research rule 6); a ledger decision follows only if the papers are silent and the cautious reading changes the design.

**C2. A weighted top tail (G-C2, C-C2, G-E3, C-E3). Adapt.**

**C3. Returns on capital and business ownership (G-C3, C-C3). Adapt: ChatGPT's portfolio approach.**

**C5. Bargaining power through a better outside option (G-E4, C-C5, C-E4). Adapt.**

## F. Fair presentation

**F1-F2 (G-F1, G-F2, C-F1, C-F2). Accept, into S7's findings page and S64's reports.**
- The S55 wealth headline is stated as a conditional model result, naming the comparison, the threshold and the assumptions, for example: "In the simulation (C2.2 design, Reference, 10,000 adults, 20 years), open PTF access left 2.42 points fewer adults with under $25,000 of real wealth than members-only access (2.40 to 2.44). The difference rests on the engine's placeholder price rule and on PTFs growing toward a 49% share at the design's at-cost prices; it disappears with a 25% ceiling, with outside running costs or with growth off. Median real wealth is far below the no-programme run's under either access rule."
- Inflation sentences follow D17 and the S39 correction: never "the pass-through lies between 0 and 1"; never "an outside model found no inflation" without its reason.
- The wealth Gini is always named "with debts counted as zero", with the median and debt measures beside it; no "inequality halved".
- The release design's null and slightly negative wealth results stay beside C2.2's.
- $25,000 is a design threshold (MODEL_SPEC), not an accepted wealth-poverty line; a threshold crossing is not a gain across the distribution.
- The price rule can bias real-wealth results either way (too much where there is slack, too little where capacity binds locally).
- The model cannot speak to power: no employer agents, no business ownership, no wage setting (until S59-S60), no politics.

# Referee Report on S82 Supply-Indexing Study

## 1. Claims that do not follow

### 1.1 "Every primary result holds under the sourced limit at every share, in every environment and at both horizons, with all three sharing rules."

**Report §2.1, §4, §11** — The decision rule (preregistration, "Decision rules 2-4") defines "holds under a supply limit" as: *the effect keeps its sign and its 95% interval excludes zero under the sourced reading with all three sharing rules*. The tables show this is true for **basket poverty** at every share. But for the other five primary outcomes (below 30 days of basic living, wealth poverty yearly/last year, median real wealth weighted/unweighted), the report itself states (§4, §11): *"The limit as built can move only basket poverty. The money a cut leaves unspent goes to other goods and the cut moves no price, so wealth, days of basic living and inflation are identical with and without it in every seed. For those outcomes, 'holds' is true by construction, not a finding."* The findings page repeats the unqualified claim ("Every primary result holds under the sourced limit..."). This overstates what was tested. The claim should be restricted to basket poverty, or explicitly noted as vacuous for the other five.

### 1.2 "With half never made up, the programme with no backing leaves more people below the basket's cost than no programme does."

**Report §2.2, §5, findings page** — The table for Reference, share 0, wIdx 0.5, wc 0 ("made up a year later: no") shows basket poverty effect **+9.58 (9.38 to 9.77)**. The no-programme level is 36.10%. The programme effect is *positive*, meaning basket poverty is *higher* with the programme than without. This is correctly reported. However, the findings page says "leaves more people below the cost of living than no programme does" — "cost of living" is not the same as "basket poverty" (fgt0PY). The outcome is basket poverty; the phrase "cost of living" conflates the two. The report uses "basket poverty" correctly; the findings page should match.

### 1.3 "At 40 years the lag compounds: with half never made up, the break-even share for basket poverty is 0.38 in Reference, 0.30 in Adverse and 0.49 in Stress"

**Report §5** — The Question 2 tables provided are **only for 20 years**. The 40-year Question 2 tables are not in the supplied material. The report quotes 40-year break-even shares for wage readings that are not in the tables given. I cannot verify these figures. If they come from the 40-year run (which was executed), they should be in a 40-year Question 2 table. The referee must ask for those tables or the report must flag that 40-year wage results are not shown here.

### 1.4 "At 40 years in Reference, median real wealth breaks even at 0.17-0.21 with a yearly delay and 0.28-0.31 with two years; no other reading does."

**Report §6** — Same issue: the Question 3 (BU delay) tables provided are only for 20 years. The 40-year BU-delay break-even shares are not in the supplied material.

### 1.5 "The joint change in basket poverty is 8.4-8.6 points... The yearly delay accounts for 81-93% of that."

**Report §7** — The Question 4 (factorial) tables are **not provided** in the supplied material. The report quotes Shapley shares and interaction sizes that I cannot verify from the given tables. The referee must ask for the Q4 tables.

### 1.6 "With the sourced limit, the market's rule cuts the poorest fifth 1.51% and the richest 0.16-0.17%."

**Report §8** — The Question 5 table shown (Reference, 20 years, no backing) gives: market's rule supply cut, Fifth 1 = 1.22, Fifth 5 = 0.13. The report says 1.51% and 0.16-0.17%. ### 1.7 "Ageing at full backing... basket poverty −8.81 points... At 40 years... −15.4 points of basket poverty and −$159,312 of median wealth."

**Report §9** — The Question 6 (smaller readings) tables are **not provided** in the supplied material. The ageing-at-full-backing figures are not verifiable here. The report says "Adverse and Stress are in the tables" but those tables are not in the packet.

## 3. The supply limit

### 3.1 Is the limit stated clearly and prominently enough?

**Yes.** The report (§4, §11), the preregistration ("Limits stated in advance"), and the findings page all state: *"The limit as built moves only basket poverty. The money a cut leaves unspent goes to other goods, and the cut moves no price."* The preregistration lists this as a stated limit (item 1). The checks confirm: *"Under every A row, below 30 days of basic living, wealth poverty... median real wealth... the change is 0 in every seed (the limit acts on these only through prices, and it moves no price)."* This is transparent.

### 3.2 Does it undermine "the results hold under a supply limit"?

**Yes, for five of six primary outcomes.** The decision rule defines "holds" per outcome per share. For basket poverty, it is a substantive test (interval excludes zero, sign kept). For the other five, the test passes vacuously because the limit does not touch them. The report acknowledges this (§4: *"For these five readings the test above passes by construction"*). The findings page should not say "Every primary result holds under the sourced limit" without qualification. It should say: "Basket poverty holds under the sourced limit; the other five primary outcomes are unaffected by the limit as built."

## 4. The wage readings

### 4.1 Is the report's reading correct and fairly framed?

**Mostly correct, with one framing issue.** The report (§5) says: *"If wages follow half of programme inflation and the rest is never made up, the programme with no backing leaves basket poverty 9.6 points higher than no programme (Reference; 5.0 in Adverse, 9.6 in Stress)."* The tables confirm:
- Reference, share 0, wIdx 0.5, wc 0: **+9.58 (9.38 to 9.77)**
- Adverse, share 0, wIdx 0.5, wc 0: **+4.99 (4.81 to 5.18)**
- Stress, share 0, wIdx 0.5, wc 0: **+9.63 (9.46 to 9.79)**

The phrase "worse than no programme at no backing when half is never made up" (in the referee's heading) matches the data. The report frames it as "the programme with no backing leaves more people below the basket's cost than no programme does" — accurate for basket poverty.

**However:** The report says "Inflation runs 146-190 points a year" — the tables show:
- Reference: 145.62
- Adverse: 160.74
- Stress: not in the provided Q2 Stress table (cut off), but likely ~190.

The real wage at year 20 falls to $22,246 (Ref) vs $94,430 (full indexing) and $92,205 (no programme). This is correctly reported.

### 4.2 Anything implausible suggesting a defect?

**The wage equation itself is a design parameter** (preregistration: *"Wage readings are design parameters: no source was found for how wages respond to an inflation the programme itself makes."*). The equation: `wg += wIdx × inflation(t) + wc × (1 − wIdx) × inflation(t − 1)`. With `wIdx=0.5, wc=0`, wages follow half of current programme inflation and the other half is **never** made up. This means real wages fall continuously as programme inflation accumulates. At share 0, programme inflation is ~40-49 pts/yr, so wages follow ~20-24.5 pts/yr. Over 20 years, the cumulative gap is large. The real wage collapsing to ~$22k (from ~$94k) is a mechanical consequence of the specification. It is not a coding defect; it is the specified reading. However, the **magnitude** (real wage falling below no-programme level) is a strong result that should be stressed: the programme **harms** the median worker's real wage at no backing under this reading. The report notes this (§5: *"the mean wage at year 20 falls to $22,246 a year in Year-0 prices, against $94,430 with full indexing and $92,205 with no programme"*). This is fair.

**One concern:** The no-programme run has **no programme inflation**, so its wages are unaffected (check 4 passes). The comparison is between a programme that generates high inflation and wages that don't keep up, vs a no-programme world with low inflation. This is the intended counterfactual. No defect detected.

## 5. The ageing reading withheld (Amendment 6)

### 5.1 Is withholding it the right call under the preregistration?

**Yes.** The preregistration (Amendment 6) states: *"The ageing reading at no backing is not reported as a finding. Its figures stay in the published tables... marked... The ageing reading at full backing is reported, labelled provisional until the rerun."* The reason: wages outrun prices many times over under ageing + programme inflation at no backing (mean wage $655k Ref, $1.1M Adv, $1.37M Str at year 20; $260B at year 40), while with no programme or at full backing (low inflation) ageing reads normally. The defect is in how the engine's ageing (v5.2 step 4) meets programme inflation — it predates S82 and is not one of its switches. Ageing is off in every other row. This meets the preregistration's decision rule 1 spirit: a defect found in a non-S82 component, isolated to one reading, with a plan to fix and rerun (PLAN.md step S91). Withholding the no-backing ageing result as a "finding" is correct; keeping the figures in the tables (marked) satisfies research rule 2.

### 5.2 Is the reason sound?

**Yes.** The evidence: one-seed trace shows the cause is the ageing option (`ag`), not the labour-force flows (`lf.fl` 'panel'). With no programme or at full backing (inflation ~0.15 pts/yr), ageing rows read normally. The defect is specific to the interaction of ageing with high programme inflation. It is not an S82 switch. The fix is scheduled separately. This is sound.

## 7. Anything else a referee would ask

### 7.1 Missing tables
The following preregistered tables are **not in the supplied material** but are quoted in the report:

- Question 3 (BU delay) — 20-year and 40-year horizons
- Question 4 (factorial) — all horizons

**The referee must require these tables be appended or linked before publication.** The report makes specific numerical claims from them (40-year break-evens, Shapley shares, ageing full-backing figures, Adverse/Stress shortfall distributions) that cannot be verified.

### 7.2 The "holds" language for non-basket-poverty outcomes
The findings page and report §2.1 say "Every primary result holds under the sourced limit." As noted in §3.2, this is vacuously true for 5 of 6 outcomes. The findings page should be corrected to: "Basket poverty holds under the sourced limit at every share... The other five primary outcomes are mechanically unchanged by the limit as built (the cut moves no price and unspent money goes to other goods)."

### 7.3 The wage lag "never made up" reading — is it plausible?
The preregistration acknowledges: *"Wage readings are design parameters: no source was found for how wages respond to an inflation the programme itself makes."* The report should explicitly flag that the "never made up" readings are **extreme bounds**, not realistic scenarios. The "made up a year later" readings are more plausible. The findings page presents both without this framing. A sentence is needed: "The 'never made up' wage readings are extreme bounds; the 'made up a year later' readings are more plausible."

### 7.4 The BU delay — design's monthly/quarterly vs yearly/two-year
The preregistration (decision rule 6) says: *"Q3's monthly and quarterly readings (the design's) are reported first, then the yearly, then the two-year bound. A sentence about indexing names the delay."* The report §6 does this. The findings page table lists them in order: month, quarter, year, two years. Good.

### 7.5 The strict bound — "not the economy and not the design"
The preregistration (decision rule 4) requires: *"The strict bound is in every table, labelled 'the strictest case: no essentials beyond today's needs consumption; not the economy and not the design'. No headline is taken from it alone, and no statement about supply limits leaves it out."* The report §4 tables label it "The strictest case: no essentials beyond today's needs consumption; not the economy and not the design". The findings page table labels it "The strictest case: no essentials beyond today's needs consumption; not the economy and not the design; the market's rule". The report §2.1 says "At the strict bound... it is at most 3.7 points smaller at 20 years and 5.1 at 40." This complies.

### 7.6 The restricted reading — "today's land-use, residency and farm policies"
The report (§4 table header) says "Restricted (today's land-use, residency and farm policies; every sourced value halved)". The preregistration (Amendment 1, item 3) says the restricted reading halves every sourced value. The label "today's land-use..." is an interpretation. The report should clarify this is a **policy reading** (halved sourced values), not a direct measurement of current policies.

### 7.7 The building speed sweep — "slowest building speed swept for medical care and food (0 a year, which puts those two sectors at the strict bound)"
The report §4 says this. The Amendment 3 table shows speed 0 = strict bound for medical/food. The sourced speed is 0.25. The decision rule table shows "holds at the strict bound for medical care and food (speed 0)" for all shares/outcomes. This is consistent.

### 7.8 Theta sweep — half and double
The report §4 says at half theta the sourced limit costs 0.48 pts (Ref, no backing, market); at double, 0.25. The Amendment 4 table confirms: half theta, sourced/pay change at share 0 = 0.48; double theta = 0.25. Good.

### 7.9 The "realised cut runs at about (T−1)/T of the target's mean"
The report §4 footnote and Amendment 5 explain this: year 0 has no cut, last target is for a year never run. The limit's own readings table shows realised cut < target cut (e.g., strict bound share 0: target 12.55%, realised 12.02%). This is correctly explained.

### 7.10 The no-programme run is never short
Preregistration limit 4: *"The no-programme run is never short. Its poorest go without through prices, as today."* The Q5 table confirms: "No programme: need nobody paid for" shows shortfall (54.26% for poorest fifth) but this is **unmet need at market prices**, not a supply cut. The supply limit does not apply to the no-programme run (check 4). This is correctly implemented.

### 7.11 The surge protocol not modelled — "cautious side"
Preregistration limit 8: *"The design's surge protocol's luxury restrictions and conversion limits are not modelled (cautious side)."* The report §11 limit 8 repeats this. This means the supply limit may be **overstated** (more shortage than if surge protocol worked). The report should note this explicitly when discussing the strict bound.

### 7.12 Building capacity costs nothing
Preregistration limit 9: *"Building capacity costs nothing in the model's accounts. The Creative Collectives' lending and output per hour are the next engine step (S85)."* The report §11 limit 9 repeats this. This means the building speed sweep is a **physical** sweep, not a fiscal one. The report should clarify that the "slowest building speed" result assumes costless capacity expansion.

### 7.13 The BU's two-year cycle
Report §11 limit 5: *"The BU's relief to the cost of living runs a damped two-year cycle in every programme row, the main row included. It predates this study and is its own inspection step."* Amendment 5 notes this. This is a known model dynamic, not an S82 result. It should be flagged as a model feature, not a finding.

### 7.14 Population fixed except ageing
Preregistration limit 7: *"The population is fixed in every row but the ageing reading."* Report §11 limit 7 repeats this. This means no demographic change in the main results. Noted.

### 7.15 Duke's views kept apart
Preregistration limit 10 and report §11 limit 10: *"Duke's views on supply policy... are his and are kept apart from these results. Nothing here tests them."* The report does not attribute supply results to Duke. Good.

### 7.16 Multiple comparisons — Holm correction

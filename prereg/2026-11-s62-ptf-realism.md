# Preregistration: PTF realism, sourced running costs, PTF jobs and wages, and the density tests (S62: switch `PTF_OPS`)

Committed Nov 14, 2026 by S62, before the switch is built and before any run. Engine: compassionism-simulation at commit `8621003f30025b5cef54fe45bf4dbef288c43887` (main); the switch lands on branch `claude/s62-ptf-realism` from it, off by default (guard test identical). The engine commit of the pilot is recorded in an amendment before the pilot runs.

## Why
- The Hub prices a PTF (a not-for-profit community business: grocers, utilities, child care) at "direct costs plus minimal cooperative overhead", so a food Basic Unit (BU) buys 2.64 times its face value at mature scale (the BLEI paper, section 3.3: PTF price 37.8% of the market price) and a utility BU 1.80 times. Both outside reviews (A3 of `audits/2026-11-s39-s55-review-triage.md`) say not-for-profit status removes outside dividends, not wages, rent, losses, depreciation or reserves, so the open-access growth rule's operations limit cannot bind as built.
- Duke answered the two design questions this step builds (ledger, recorded Nov 9): **D19**, when PTF prices sit below full cost the Source pays the gap as an explicit subsidy, counted in programme cost; **D20**, the 40% and 55% density tests are replaced by a smooth curve, with the hard steps reported beside it as the papers' wording.
- The Hub gives the conversion-rate bonus (1.30 plus θ) to PTF *workers* ("PTF workers receive elevated CCO conversion rates as an employment incentive"); the engine has no PTF workforce, so S55 kept the bonus with members.

## What the engine already does (read before this file; not a result of any run)
- Programme cost already counts the PTF price cut as a cost, less the part a PTF funds from forgone profit (plan step 5: BEA 2025 net operating surplus less depreciation, 4.94% of food sales and 20.3% of utility sales). So the engine's cost accounting has been, in effect, D19's subsidy at that cost level since step 5; what was missing is a reserve, the Hub's own cost structure as a reading, the price-rise reading, and sourced costs by sector.
- θ (the synergy coefficient) is already a continuous ramp: 0 below 55%, rising linearly to 0.25 at 90% (`szhTheta`). The 55% "test" is a kink, not a jump. The food ε is a jump: 1.65 below the 40% test, 2.64 at or above it.
- Under open access (S55) every adult buys the zone's PTF share s of food and utilities at PTF prices; the tests read s; the operations limit has a main reading `'hub'` (never binds) and a sensitivity `'outside'` (no payer: no zone grows).

## Claude's reading of the design (the Hub papers, read for this step)
- The BLEI paper derives ε from a cost structure: "direct variable costs (production, logistics, perishables) at approximately 35–40% of consumer price", conventional overhead 20–25%, shareholder return 15–20%; PTFs "eliminate shareholder return entirely and reduce overhead by 40–55%", so ε = 1/(0.350 + 0.028) = 2.64 at mature scale, 2.22 "conservative; early-phase", 1.50–2.00 "during establishment". Section 6.1: "Below approximately 40% PTF merchant participation in a given zone, ε falls toward 1.5–1.8×." The paper does not say who pays if those costs are not met; D19 answers that.
- Outside data disagree with the cost structure by a wide margin: US food and beverage stores pay about 71 cents of every sales dollar for the goods they resell (Census's 28.8% gross margin) and about 26 cents in operating expenses (below). A grocer cannot sell at 37.8% of the market price on those costs. The model of the design keeps the Hub's prices (D19: the Source pays the gap) and counts the gap.
- "PTF workers": who works at a PTF is not said. Cautious reading (a modelling assumption): PTF jobs are spread over the zone's workers like the sector's jobs, so each worker's share of hours at PTFs is the zone's PTF share of sales times the sector's share of all jobs; the bonus applies on that share of the worker's conversion rate. Members as such lose the bonus (they keep everything else membership gives).
- The smooth curve (D20): the papers give the low value (1.65), the mature value (2.64) and "approximately 40%". Reading: the PTF's cost share of the market price, 1/ε, moves linearly from 1/1.65 to 1/2.64 across a band centred on 40%, so the step is the band's limit as its width goes to zero and the curve neither adds to nor takes from the step on average across the band. The band's half-width w is a labelled design parameter: 0.20 main (20% to 60%), swept 0.10 and 0.40. θ is already continuous and is kept as it is.

## The mechanism (switch `PTF_OPS`, row option `po`; off = identical; needs open access, `pa`, and PTF by sector)
1. **Running costs (`cost`)**: a PTF's running cost per market dollar of sales, by sector.
   - `'full'` (main): full-cost break-even for a not-for-profit whose capital comes from the reinvestment account: the sector's costs without what owners, lenders and income tax take (1 − BEA's net operating surplus less depreciation: food 0.9506, utilities 0.797), capital replacement included, plus a reserve `res` (0.017, FMI's net margin, what a going concern keeps; swept 0): food 0.9676, utilities 0.814.
   - `'sector'`: observed costs and margins: food, the stores' cost of goods plus operating expenses (1 − 0.288 + 0.2603 = 0.9723, Census); utilities, public power's price relative to investor-owned (0.839, EIA-861).
   - `'hub'`: the Hub's cost structure as stated: the cost equals the PTF price (1/ε), so there is no gap.
2. **Who pays (`pay`, D19)**: `'source'` (main): PTF prices stay at the Hub's ε; the gap between the PTF price and the running cost is a subsidy from the Source, counted in programme cost (the forgone-profit part, which costs nothing, is market price less running cost). `'price'`: prices rise to running cost where they sit below it, so the PTF cut is 1 − running cost per market dollar (food 3.2%, utilities 18.6% under `'full'`), with no density test, no SZH addition to the cut, and no cut on other sectors; no subsidy. Under `'hub'` costs nothing is paid. The operations limit of the growth rule never binds under either payer (the Source covers the gap, or prices cover cost); S55's `'outside'` (no payer) is refused with `po`.
3. **The PTF workforce (`bonus`)**: `'workers'` (main): each worker (an adult with a wage who is not retired) works the share k × s of their hours at PTFs, k = 0.02423 (food and beverage stores plus utilities over all jobs, CES 2024) and s their zone's PTF share of sales; their conversion-rate bonus is 1 + k s (1.30 + θ − 1). `'members'`: as S55 (the bonus on members). Reported: PTF jobs per 100 adults, the PTF share of workers' hours, the mean bonus.
4. **PTF wages**: set as every wage is, by S60's wage setting (`bg`) where the row has it: main pass-through 0 (no change), 0.03 beside it. With a bargained raise the PTF's payroll per market dollar (food 0.1035, utilities 0.1138; Census payroll over revenue) rises by the year's earnings-weighted mean raise, which raises the running cost and so the subsidy (or, under `'price'`, the price).
5. **Staff as well as capital (`hire`)**: `null` (main): no hiring limit; the PTFs' staff are the sector's workers moving with its sales, which no share below 100% exhausts. A hiring-speed limit is a labelled design parameter with no source: the population's PTF share may rise at most `hire` a year (staff are proportional to sales); swept 0.05 and 0.02, called stress readings.
6. **The density tests**: `curve` `'smooth'` (main, D20; band half-width `w` 0.20, swept 0.10 and 0.40) or `'step'` (the papers' wording); `test`, what the 40% and 55% tests read: `'sales'` (main, S55: the zone's PTF share of sales), `'members'` (the zone's share of adults who are members), `'capacity'` (the share of demand the PTFs' capacity can serve: min(1, capacity / demand ceiling), population-wide). Under `'price'` ε no longer reads density.
7. **Sector-specific demand ceilings**: none built. No source found gives a ceiling for open-access not-for-profit utilities or child care (public power's 15% and cooperatives' 13% of customers are where they exist, not ceilings); S55's one ceiling (0.49) and its 0.25-1 sweep stand, 0.75 and 1 called stress tests.
- `po {cost:'hub', bonus:'members', curve:'step', test:'sales'}` must equal the row without `po` on every output (unit test and pilot row `hub0`). No random draw is added (CRN holds). The no-programme run is untouched (open access needs PTF).

## What is reported (keys `PO_KEYS`, rows with `po` only)
`poSub` the PTF subsidy (the PTF cut less its forgone-profit part, $ a year per adult, year-0 dollars); `poSubSh` its share of programme cost (%); `poFree` the forgone-profit part; `poEpsF` the mean food ε at which adults buy (over adult-years, PTF purchases weighted by s); `poCov` PTF sales at PTF prices over running cost (%); `poWk` PTF jobs per 100 adults; `poWkH` the PTF share of workers' hours (%); `poBon` the mean conversion-rate bonus over workers who convert (multiplier − 1, %); `poHire` the share of years from year 1 in which the hiring limit binds (%); `poG` the PTF payroll raise from bargaining (%).

## Unit tests (written with the build)
1. Off and `hub0` identical to the row without `po`. 2. Costs by hand: the subsidy per market dollar under `'full'` and `'sector'` on food and utilities at a fixed share equals the hand calculation; under `'hub'` the subsidy is 0 and programme cost falls by the PTF cut less the step-5 free part. 3. `'price'`: the cut equals 1 − running cost on food and utilities at any share; the subsidy is 0. 4. The smooth curve: equals the step outside the band, linear in 1/ε inside, and as w → 0 equals the step. 5. Workers: the bonus is 1 + k s (pcb − 1) on a worker, 1 on a retiree and on a non-worker, independent of membership; with `bonus:'members'` the S55 rule. 6. The test readings: `'members'` reads the zone's member share; `'capacity'` reads min(1, c/dem). 7. The hiring limit: the population's share rises at most `hire` a year and binds where it should. 8. Wages: with `bg` π 0 nothing changes; with a positive π the running cost rises by payroll share × the mean raise. 9. Refused settings (no open access, `ops:'outside'`, unknown values). 10. No random draw.

## Pilot (100 seeds, 500 adults, 20 years; Reference, Adverse, Stress; the release row and the C2.2 design against no programme on the same seeds)
Base row: S60's (S71's rows, adults outside the labour force) with S60's wage setting at its main reading (`bg` π 0) and S55's open access with growth (`pa: 'open'`, main readings). Readings: `open` (no `po`); `hub0` (must equal `open`); `main` (`po` main); `hubSt` (the Hub's costs as stated); `price` (D19's price-rise reading); `sector`; `res0`; `members` (bonus on members); `step`; `w10`; `w40`; `tMem`; `tCap`; `hire5`; `hire2`; `bg03` (main with π 0.03). Outcomes: basket poverty (`fgt0PY`), weighted wealth poverty (`wtPovE`), weighted top 1% wealth share (`wtTop1`) where reported, the cost of living, the price level, programme cost and its parts (`cost`, `cCut`, `csFree`, `cConv`), the PTF share of sales (`paSh`, `paS19`), the `PO_KEYS`; paired effects with 2.5-97.5 percentile intervals of 2,000 bootstrap replicates over seeds, unadjusted (a pilot is a mechanism check, not a result). Runs on the session machine in resumable chunks; results to compassionism-sim-results `s62-ptf-ops-<engine commit>` with run.txt and SHA256SUMS.

## Decision rules (fixed now)
1. **Checks.** Unit tests and the guard test pass; `hub0` equals `open` on every kept key in every environment. If not, the switch is not recommended for the freeze until fixed.
2. **The subsidy** (D19) is reported in dollars and as a share of programme cost for every reading and design, with no threshold; it is also reported against the step-5 accounting (`open`), so the change from adding the reserve and the sector figures is visible.
3. **Signs.** For each reading, the five outcomes above (basket poverty, wealth poverty, the cost of living, the price level, programme cost) are compared in sign with `main`; any sign change of a programme effect is stated in the report's first section.
4. **Recommendation to the design freeze (S63), fixed now, subject to rule 1:** `po` at its main reading in the main row (full cost, the Source pays the gap, the bonus on PTF workers, the smooth curve at w 0.20, the tests reading sales, no hiring limit). Beside it on every outcome: `price` (D19's other option, the reading with no subsidy), `hubSt` (the Hub's costs as stated), `step` (the papers' wording, D20). To S70's screen: `sector`, `res0`, `w10`, `w40`, `tMem`, `tCap`, `hire5`, `hire2`, `members`. S63 chooses.
5. No synergy is claimed: these rows are not a factorial design.

## Limits known before the build
- One running cost per sector for every zone and every share; scale economies enter only through the Hub's ε (smooth curve or step).
- PTF jobs are spread over all workers, not given to particular adults; no job is created or lost (PTF jobs replace the private sector's jobs with its sales).
- The hiring limit has no source; it is a stress reading only.
- Utilities' sector reading is electricity only (EIA-861); gas and water are read at the same ratio.
- Child care has sourced costs (Census, CRS) but no PTF share in the engine's basket (`other` sectors are 0 in every main row); its figures are recorded, not used.
- Tested with S60's rows, open access and `bg` only; S68's pairwise tests cover the rest.

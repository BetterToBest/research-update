# Preregistration: S70, which assumptions move the headline (the round-2 screen)

Committed Nov 14, 2026 by S70, before any run of the screen. Engine: compassionism-simulation at commit `8b96cdd1c6c14c0bef834ccc8a0144cf86036098` (main, after S69; no engine change in this step). Job script: `runner/jobs/s70-screen.js` in compassionism-sim-results (its pull request is named in Amendment 1 with its merge commit, before dispatch).

Before this file, the job script was run only as a code test: its design check (`--check`), its self-test (`--selftest`, seeds 1-2, Reference: every setter at the main row's reading reproduces the untouched main pair on all 2,880 series, largest difference 0; each input moved alone from the grid's low corner) and a timing run of 8 design points on seeds 1-2. No elementary effect was computed or read.

## Why
Round 2 has many readings, each tested one at a time in its own step (S55-S73). S63 must choose which of them the 500-seed batch carries beside the main row, and S64-S65 must show how far each headline depends on them. One-at-a-time tests miss readings that matter only together. This screen moves every swept reading together and ranks them by how much they move each headline outcome (PLAN S70 items 1-2). It also designs the specification curve that S63 preregisters (item 3).

## Questions
- **Q1.** For each headline outcome, design, backing reading and environment: which readings move the programme's effect most when all readings vary together? Answer: the inputs ranked by μ* (below), with the noise check.
- **Q2.** Which of them act through other readings (interaction or curvature)? Answer: the flag of the Analysis section.
- **Q3.** Which readings must S63's batch carry, which may it carry at one reading, and which may it hold at the main row? Answer: the decision rules below, applied mechanically.

## Design
- **Method.** Morris elementary effects (Morris 1991; Campolongo, Cariboni and Saltelli 2007), as `dev/tools/gsa.js` (v5.4) ran them: R = 12 random trajectories per block; each input on a 4-level grid of its range (u = 0, 1/3, 2/3, 1), step 2/3 of the range; each trajectory starts on the grid's lower half and moves the inputs one at a time in a random order. Generator seeds 70001 (block S) and 70002 (block F). Continuous inputs read `lo + u (hi − lo)`; integer inputs are rounded; categorical inputs list the main reading first: two levels read u < 1/2 as the first (so every step switches from the main reading to the other), three levels read the grid's levels 0-3 as A, B, B, C (a step goes A→B or B→C).
- **The row.** The candidate main row (`r2MainRow`, `r2MainPair`, `r2MainStudy`), applied to both runs of a pair; every input below is a row option set on both runs (the financing options on the programme run only, as S72's pilot). 500 adults, 20 years.
- **Block S, the Source rows (33 inputs, 408 design points).** Five rows per point: the no-programme run; the C2.2 design and the release design at full backing (`o.a` 1: Duke's decision of Nov 14, the main row); both designs at no assumed backing (`o.a` 0: only the output the model sees sold matches the payout, the backing curve's low end and the reading S69 checked). The backed share is not an input (PLAN S70, from the S69 session: it moves inflation by construction and would swamp the ranking; S63 carries it as its own curve). At full backing almost no new money reaches prices, so the price rule's readings can matter only at no assumed backing; both are screened so that S63 sees each end.
- **Block F, the financed rows (41 inputs, 504 design points).** Three rows per point: the no-programme run, the C2.2 design and the release design paid for by a contribution (`o.fin 'tax'`), the rest met as `fx.fin` says (S72; D23's rows lie inside the box). Block F runs without the weighted tail in both runs (S72 Amendment 2: the bases 'wc' and 'all' are not read with the tail on); the tail's three inputs are screened in block S only.
- **Environments:** Reference and Adverse (PLAN S70). Stress is not screened: S63's batch carries it for every reading the screen keeps, and every reading the screen drops is also dropped only on Reference and Adverse evidence (stated beside each).
- **Seeds:** 1-40, paired across every row and point (common random numbers). Two blocks for the noise check: 1-20 and 21-40.
- **Where it runs:** GitHub Actions, the `run-study` workflow of compassionism-sim-results (`prereg/RUNNING.md`): studies `s70-screen-s` and `s70-screen-f`, engine as above, job `s70-screen.js`, job_args `--block=S --env=both --procs=4` (and `--block=F`), seeds `1-40`, 20 shards. Results folders `s70-screen-s-8b96cdd/` and `s70-screen-f-8b96cdd/`. Size: 163,200 and 120,960 runs, about 23 and 17 core-hours (0.5 s a run, timed).

## Outcomes
- **Primary (the five headline outcomes):** the programme's effect, programme run minus its own no-programme run, mean over seeds: basic living (`bNAPy`, adult-years below 30 days of basic living, design-neutral, points); the cost of living (`fgt0PY`, adult-years with income below the basket, points); wealth poverty as a yearly average (`ppovA`, the mean of `ppov_1`…`ppov_19`, points; unweighted, as S73 read it: the tail's 12 adults count at weight 1); median real wealth (`wtMedE`, weighted median at the end of year 20, year-0 dollars); programme inflation (`endoAnn`, points a year).
- **Block F also:** programme debt at year 20 (`gvDebtY`, % of GDP) and the year it passes GDP (`gvY100`); for these two the effect at a point is the median over seeds, not the mean (debt explodes in some readings; PLAN S70 from S72).
- **Secondary (reported, not ranked):** wealth poverty at year 20 (`pov`), programme cost (`cost`), the contribution's mean rate (`tauMean`).
- Hours are not an outcome of this screen, so S68's note on the hours response and population size does not bear on it.

## Analysis
- **Elementary effect.** For trajectory t and the input moved at a step: EE = effect at the new point − effect at the point before (same seeds), in the outcome's units, for one step of 2/3 of the range (a categorical input: its switch). Per input: μ* = mean |EE| over the 12 trajectories (the ranking), μ = mean EE (its sign), σ = standard deviation of EE.
- **Cells.** Block S: 2 designs × 2 backing readings × 2 environments × 5 outcomes = 40 rankings. Block F: 2 designs × 2 environments × 7 outcomes = 28.
- **Noise check (as `gsa.js morrisjoin`).** The same trajectories on seeds 1-20 and 21-40: an input's μ* counts as measured when the two blocks' μ* differ by less than a quarter of the joined μ*. Seed noise in σ: v = mean over trajectories of (EE₁ − EE₂)²/4 is the noise variance of the joined EE; σ_adj = √max(0, σ² − v).
- **Interaction flag.** σ_adj ≥ 0.5 μ*: the input's effect depends on where the other readings are (interaction, or curvature for a continuous input; for a two-level input it can only be interaction). σ_adj ≥ μ*: strong. These are flags for S63's design, not synergy claims (research rule 4 applies to the batch's factorial contrasts with intervals, not to the screen).
- **No headline figure comes from the screen.** Its points are off the main row and its seeds are few; it ranks, it does not estimate.

## Decision rules (fixed now; applied mechanically in the note)
1. **Materiality thresholds.** 1 point for basic living, the cost of living and wealth poverty (the study programme's materiality, `prereg/2026-11-study-program.md` rule 2, and S73's rules C-D). For the others, this screen's choice, fixed now: 1 point a year of programme inflation; 5% of the no-programme median real wealth (the mean over the screen's no-programme rows in that environment, so about $10,000); for debt, 10% of GDP at year 20 and 2 years in the year debt passes GDP.
2. **Carry in full.** An input whose μ* reaches the threshold on any primary outcome in any cell: S63's batch carries it at both ends of its range (each category for a categorical input) beside the main row, and it appears on that outcome's specification curve. If its μ* is not measured (noise check) it is still carried (the cautious reading).
3. **Carry at one reading.** μ* from half the threshold up to the threshold in some cell, and below half elsewhere: S63 carries the end with the larger mean effect (the sign of μ on the trajectories that reach it), one reading.
4. **Hold at main.** μ* below half the threshold in every cell, and not flagged: S63 may hold it at the main reading. It is listed in S63's preregistration as screened out, with its largest μ* and the cell.
5. **Interactions.** An input carried under rule 2 and flagged (σ_adj ≥ 0.5 μ*): S63 also carries it crossed with the top-ranked input of the same outcome and cell (a 2 × 2 beside the curve), or states why not.
6. **Inactive inputs.** An input that cannot act at the main row (the recession slack without the spending layer; the markdown at pass-through 0; the policy-rate rule's coefficients under the fixed rate; the price rule's readings at full backing) is reported as such; rules 2-4 are read on the cells where it can act.
7. **Agreement with the one-at-a-time steps.** Where the screen ranks a reading far from its own step's one-at-a-time result (an input its step found to move a headline by over 1 point with μ* below half the threshold here, or the reverse), the note says so and S63 carries it (the cautious reading).

## The specification curve (item 3; designed here, preregistered in S63)
- **What it is** (Simonsohn, Simmons and Nelson 2020, "Specification curve analysis", Nature Human Behaviour 4): every preregistered reading's estimate of one outcome on one chart, ordered by size, each with its interval, and under the chart a panel that marks which reading each point is.
- **One curve for each** headline outcome (the five above) × design (C2.2, release) × environment (Reference, Adverse, Stress) × horizon (20 and 40 years) on the full-backing main row. The backing curve and the financed rows are separate curves (S63 items from S69 and S72), drawn the same way and never mixed into this one.
- **The points.** The main row; every input carried under rule 2 at each end (or category), the others at main; every input carried under rule 3 at its one reading; each 2 × 2 of rule 5; and one labelled bound: every carried input at its least favourable end for that outcome together (chosen by the sign of μ in the screen, fixed in S63 before the batch). Points with the same reading on every input are drawn once.
- **Each point** is the programme's effect (programme minus its own no-programme run, paired over the batch's 500 seeds) with its 95% interval (paired bootstrap, 2,000 resamples, or the paired t where S63 says so). The main row is marked; the zero line is drawn.
- **The panel** under the chart lists the carried inputs as rows and marks each point's reading (main, low end, high end, category).
- **The headline sentence** gives the range across readings first and the seed interval second, for example: "Across the 23 preregistered readings, the C2.2 design lowers basket poverty by 18 to 34 points; at the main row, 31 points (seed interval 30 to 32)." No headline figure is published with its seed interval alone (PLAN S63, from the Nov 9 audit).
- **Data files:** one CSV per curve (reading, each input's value, estimate, interval, seeds), beside the chart in compassionism-sim-results and on the findings page.

## Reporting
Every cell above is published, including inputs that move nothing and the cells where the screen finds no material reading: the shard files, `run.txt` and `SHA256SUMS` in compassionism-sim-results; the tables and the plain-words note `dev/reports/s70-screen.md` in compassionism-simulation; the readings for S63 written into PLAN S63.

## Limits stated in advance
- Morris ranks over the box the ranges span; it does not estimate the main row's effect or a reading's own effect. A range chosen wider moves an input up the ranking; every range is the cited step's own sweep.
- Twelve trajectories give a ranking, not variance shares; Sobol indices (as `gsa.js sobol`) are the follow-up if the ranking leaves a close call that changes rule 2.
- The three-level categories step A→B or B→C, never A→C; their μ* mixes the two steps.
- 500 adults; 20 years. The hours response's dependence on population size (S68) does not enter, since hours are not ranked.

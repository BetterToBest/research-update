# Preregistration: bargaining power, a worker's outside option and an employer markdown (S60: switch `BARGAIN`)

Committed Nov 13, 2026 by S60, before the switch is built and before any run. Engine: compassionism-simulation at commit `a00a24fd951529eed5e3f4b1e987b56598b5caf1` (main); the switch lands on branch `claude/s60-bargaining-power` from it, off by default (guard test identical). The engine commit of the pilot is recorded in Amendment 1 before the pilot runs.

## Why
- The engine's wage per hour does not depend on what a worker could get by not working. A Basic Unit (BU) changes what not working is worth: the reviews (C5 of `audits/2026-11-s39-s55-review-triage.md`) asked whether a better outside option lets workers bargain higher wages, and how much.
- Where employers pay less than what a worker adds (a markdown), there is room for a wage to rise without the job ceasing to pay. Where there is no markdown there is no room: a raise above what the worker adds ends the job.
- The question is a magnitude: how many cents of wage does a dollar of outside option buy, and what share of the programme's wage gains would come from it.

## What the engine already does (read before this file; not a result of any run)
- Wages grow by base growth, the BLEI bonus, the CIP term and price indexation (MODEL_SPEC 6.1). Earnings respond to the programme through the labour response (6.11): `E = E0 × R^(ε − ρ) − ρ × (unconditional dollars)`, ε 0.33 (Chetty 2012), ρ 0.16 (Vivalt et al.). Every price-cut dollar, BU spent, cash and last year's liquid PTH appreciation count as unconditional dollars; expired BU do not (`LABOR`, `tbLab`).
- So the income effect of a BU is already in the model: a worker with more unconditional dollars earns less. What is missing is any effect on the wage rate.

## Claude's reading of the design (the Hub papers, read for this step)
The papers expect the programme to remove the economic desperation that makes people accept bad terms: the Citizens Internet Portal paper (6.2.1, "Corruption exploits desperation": workers threatened with job loss, whistleblowers who "choose silence over unemployment"), and the Corporate Transformation paper scores "labor exploitation (poverty wages, poor conditions)" as an extraction category. The BLEI paper cites the Alaska dividend's negligible employment effects. No paper gives a figure for how much wages rise, or says the BU raises wages one for one. Where the papers are silent the model takes the most cautious reading: a BU raises a worker's outside option by what it buys, and that moves the wage only by the pass-through the evidence supports. This is a model of the design, not a change to it; no ledger decision is needed.

## Sources (opened for this step; figures as read)
1. **Markdowns.** Azar, Berry and Marinescu, *Estimating Labor Market Power*, NBER w30365 (2022): firm-level labour supply elasticity 4.8; median markdown (MRPL − w)/w 0.21, so the wage is 1/1.21 = 0.826 of what the worker adds. Yeh, Macaluso and Hershbein, *AER* 112(7), 2022: US manufacturing plants, average markdown MRPL/w 1.53, so 0.654.
2. **Wages and the value of not working.** Jäger, Schoefer, Young and Zweimüller, *QJE* 135(4), 2020 (NBER w25230): four Austrian unemployment-benefit reforms; wages respond by less than $0.01 per $1.00 of benefit (preferred estimates 0.00, SE 0.013, after one year; −0.027, SE 0.026, after two); increases above $0.03 are ruled out; the Nash bargaining model predicts $0.24 to $0.48.
3. **Unconditional cash in the US.** Vivalt, Rhodes, Bartik, Broockman, Krause and Miller, NBER w32719, revised Aug 2026; the NBER page lists *QJE* 141(4), 2026, 2675-2734. $1,000 a month for three years: individual income excluding the transfer −$1,900 a year (16 cents per dollar); labour-market participation −4.2 points (pooled administrative and survey data; survey −2.3, administrative −6.6); hours −1 to −2 a week. Hourly pay from all jobs −$0.14 (SE 0.37; control $17.26), increases above $0.58 an hour ruled out. The reservation wage participants report −$0.27 an hour (SE 0.50; control $18.30). Recipients were more likely to search for a job (+6 points) but applied to fewer jobs; non-employment spells 0.8 months longer; no change in job quality. *Correction to the triage (C5):* it recorded "no QJE version found" and used the January 2026 figures (3.9 points, $1,800); the August 2026 revision and its journal listing give 4.2 points and $1,900, as one review had cited.
4. **Kenya.** Egger, Haushofer, Miguel, Niehaus and Walker, *Econometrica* 2022 (NBER w26600, revised Oct 2021): hourly wages of employees in recipient households +0.04 (SE 0.04) on a mean of 0.70; in non-recipient households +0.19 (SE 0.10). The paper attributes wage gains to the demand the transfers created (a local multiplier), not to recipients' outside options.
5. **Finland.** Verho, Hämäläinen and Kanninen, *AEJ: Economic Policy* 14(1), 2022: a basic income in place of minimum unemployment benefits; no significant change in days employed in the first year. No wage measure.
6. **Alaska.** Jones and Marinescu, *AEJ: Economic Policy* 14(2), 2022: the dividend had no effect on employment; part-time work +1.8 points (17%). No wage measure.
7. **Stockton.** West and Castro Baker, first-year results released Mar 3, 2021 (Penn SP2): $500 a month for 24 months to 125 recipients; full-time employment rose faster among recipients than controls (press figures 28% to 40% against 32% to 37%). Small sample; no wage measure.

What the sources say together: no study finds that unconditional cash raises wages through the outside option. The one US experiment that asked finds no rise in reservation wages or hourly pay. The best natural experiment on the value of not working finds less than a cent per dollar. Wage gains found in Kenya come from demand, which this switch does not model.

## The mechanism (switch `BARGAIN`, row option `bg`; off = identical)
Reduced form, labelled as such: no firms, vacancies or hiring (a full employer layer is later work, as the triage decided).
- **The markdown.** A worker's wage is `m` of what they add: `MRPL = w / m`, read on the year's wage including the programme's raises (the engine takes those raises as matched by output). The room for a bargained raise is `1/m − 1` of the wage.
- **The outside option.** What the programme adds to what a worker would keep without working: the same unconditional dollars the income effect counts, at the same weights (BU spent at the worker's own prices, so the BU enters at the value of what it buys; cash of the comparison designs; price-cut dollars; last year's liquid PTH appreciation; owners' dollars; conversion proceeds as far as the income effect treats them as unearned), and not today's safety net (it is the baseline the wages were measured under). Expired BU buy the holder nothing and add nothing. With no programme the rise is zero, so a no-programme run is identical to off.
- **The bargained raise.** `g = min(π × O / E_full, 1/m − 1)`, `O` the outside-option rise in the year, `E_full` the year's full-year earnings before any response, `π` the pass-through (cents of yearly wage per dollar of yearly outside option). The raise is for the year (it goes when the outside option goes); it is not added to the wage path.
- **One wage elasticity (guardrail 7).** Earnings with the raise: the wage part `(1 + g)` and the hours response to it at the elasticity every other raise gets, so earnings are multiplied by `(1 + g)^(1 + ε − ρ)`. The income effect is unchanged (the same dollars, the same ρ). The hours measure divides the bargained wage out, so it stays hours.
- **No random draw**; adults outside the labour force, retirees and anyone with no earnings in the year get no raise.
- **Readings.** `π`: 0 (the central estimate: Jäger et al.'s preferred estimates are 0.00 and −0.027; Vivalt et al.'s points are negative), 0.03 (Jäger et al.'s upper bound), 0.10 (Vivalt et al.'s upper bound on hourly pay, $0.58 an hour, against $1,000 a month spread over 2,080 hours, $5.77 an hour), 0.24 and 0.48 (the Nash benchmarks Jäger et al. calibrate; rejected by both studies, shown as the theory case). `m`: 0.826 (main), 0.654, 1 (no markdown).

## What is reported (keys `BG_KEYS`, rows with `bg` only; workers are adults with earnings in the year; weighted by the tail's weights where the row has them)
Mean outside-option rise and bargained raise per worker-year (Year-0 dollars, years 1-19); the earnings it adds after the hours response; the share of the programme's wage gain that comes from the outside option (bargained raise over all the programme's raises to the wage rate: BLEI, octave, CIP, ESP payroll and business payout, and the bargained raise, earnings-weighted); the share of worker-years at the markdown cap; the average markdown (earnings-weighted wage over MRPL); the distribution of the outside-option rise as a share of full-year earnings (P10, P50, P90, last year); under the Nash readings, the reservation wage the Nash relation implies (`(1 − (1 − π)/m)/π` of the wage before the programme, plus the rise) at P10, P50, P90; the bargained raise against the capital income the model's households receive (a size check for who pays: see limits).

## Unit tests (written with the build)
1. Off and `π = 0` give identical results to the switch absent (every key, programme and no-programme rows).
2. `m = 1` gives identical results for any `π` (no room).
3. A no-programme row is identical to off for any `π`, `m`.
4. By hand on built cases: `g = min(π O / E_full, 1/m − 1)`; earnings multiplied by `(1 + g)^(1 + ε − ρ)`; with ε = ρ exactly `(1 + g)`.
5. The cap: no worker's wage after the raise exceeds what they add (`w (1 + g) ≤ w / m` within rounding), and with π 1 the cap binds for some workers.
6. More outside option gives a larger raise (monotone in π); the outside option counts exactly the income effect's dollars without the safety net (with ρ_BU set to 0, BU drop out of it).
7. The draw count is unchanged (eight draws per agent-year).
8. The hours measure is unchanged by the raise when the labour response has ε = ρ.

## Pilot (100 seeds, 500 adults, 20 years; Reference, Adverse, Stress; the release row and the C2.2 design against no programme on the same seeds)
Base row: S71's rows (frozen row, today's safety net, CPS earnings, wealth by asset class with owners and capital income, the tail, the creditor side under its main reading) with adults outside the labour force (`lf`, S61's main-row candidate), since an outside option means little while every adult works. Readings: `off`; `bg0` (π 0, m 0.826; must equal `off`); `bg03` (0.03); `bg10` (0.10); `nash24` (0.24); `nash48` (0.48); `nash48y` (0.48, m 0.654); `nash48n` (0.48, m 1; must equal `off`). Outcomes: the `BG_KEYS`, wage earnings, hours, employment, basket poverty, wealth poverty and the top 1% share (weighted), inflation, the cost of living; paired effects with 2.5-97.5 percentile intervals of 2,000 bootstrap replicates over seeds, unadjusted (a pilot is a mechanism check, not a result).

**Validation against Vivalt et al.** No-programme world, the base row, three years: $12,000 a year to every adult (unfinanced) against nothing, same seeds (100), Reference. Reported: earnings excluding the transfer per adult and per dollar (study: −$1,900, 16 cents), the share of working-age adults with earnings (study: −4.2 points), the hours measure, and the bargained hourly pay change under each π against the study's interval (−$0.87 to +$0.59 an hour).

## Decision rules (fixed now)
1. **Checks.** Unit tests and the guard test pass; `bg0` and `nash48n` equal `off` on every kept key in every environment. If not, the switch is not recommended for the freeze until fixed.
2. **Evidence test.** A reading is "within the evidence" only if π ≤ 0.03 (Jäger et al.) and its validation pay change lies inside Vivalt et al.'s interval; π 0.10 is the outer edge of the US experiment alone; π 0.24 and 0.48 are labelled "theory readings the evidence rejects" wherever shown.
3. **What share of the wage gain comes from the outside option** is reported for every reading and design, with no threshold.
4. **Hours and participation.** The engine's validation response is compared with the study's. Earnings per dollar within 5 cents of 16 cents passes. If the engine's participation change is less than half the study's 4.2 points, the gap is reported as a limit (the engine puts the response on hours, not on who works) and listed for S70's screen and S63; it is not fixed in this step.
5. **Recommendation to the design freeze (S63), fixed now, subject to rule 1:** `BARGAIN` at π 0 in the main row (identical to off: the central estimate), with π 0.03 beside it on every wage, income and poverty outcome, and π 0.10 as the outer evidence reading; the Nash readings go to S70's screen as the theory case, not to S63 as main-row candidates. The markdown 0.826 main, 0.654 and 1 swept. S63 chooses.
6. No synergy is claimed: these rows are not a factorial design.

## Limits known before the build
- Reduced form: one markdown for every worker, no firms, no vacancies, no job-to-job moves.
- Who pays a raise is not modelled: it comes out of the employer's markdown (owners' profits) or prices. The raise is reported in dollars against the model's capital income, so its size is visible; a payer is needed before any reading above 0.03 is used for a wealth result.
- The unemployment-benefit evidence measures a dollar paid only when not working; a BU is paid whether or not one works, so in standard models it moves the outside option less. Jäger et al.'s bound is if anything generous for a BU.
- Wage gains from the demand the programme creates (Kenya) are not this mechanism; in the engine wages follow prices (indexation) and the spending layer fills recession slack only.
- The engine's labour response has no separate participation margin (rule 4).
- Tested with S71's rows and `lf` only; S68's pairwise tests cover the rest.

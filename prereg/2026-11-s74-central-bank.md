Committed Nov 14, 2026 by S74, before the engine change and before any run. Engine: compassionism-simulation at commit `cabe44c` (main, S63/S69 merged). The changes land on branch `claude/s74-central-bank`, every part behind a switch that is off by default (guard test identical). Round 2's frozen engine (`104691f`, S64's batch) and release 2 are untouched. The engine commit of the pilot is recorded in an amendment before the pilot runs. Timing: ledger D24, answered by Duke on Nov 14 (start now, while S64's batch runs).

## Why
Neither run has a central bank. Outside inflation is a fixed rate (0% Reference, 2% Adverse and Stress). Savings earn a fixed real rate (0.97%), and home and share prices rise at fixed long-run averages (S59). The policy rate appears only through the deposit pass-through (0.46, Drechsler, Savov and Schnabl 2017). So the yardstick leaves out how monetary policy moves wealth between groups. The programme run also leaves out the response a central bank would make to the programme's own inflation (21-33% a year in S68's C2.2 row financed by new money). This step adds both sides, and it tests the design's stated aim of stability (PLAN.md S74, Duke's note of Nov 13) in both directions.

## What the engine does today (read before this file; not a result of any run)
- Outside inflation `p.inflRate`, fixed per environment. Recessions: `buildRecessionPath` (a drawn path), or a fixed path `REC_SCRIPT` (session 9's history check, 2008-2012 and 2020-2021 only).
- With asset classes on (`rt`, in the round-2 main row; MODEL_SPEC 14.6), each year: real classes move by `(1 + π)(1 + ρ_c) − 1` (ρ home 0.94%, shares and business 4.02%, Shiller 1953-2022). Nominal assets move by `0.46 π`, floating debt by `1 × π`, and fixed-rate debt by `0 × π`. Real assets are never sold. A shortfall draws nominal assets to zero, then borrows at the floating rate.
- Job loss (MODEL_SPEC 12.1): a yearly chance of 8.51%, or 14.23% in a recession year (CPS flows). The recession is an on/off flag per year with an income multiplier.
- S72's government account already holds a policy-rate reading for the programme's borrowing (`gv.r 'rule'`). This step's rule is the economy-wide one. Where both are on, S72's reading takes this step's rate (unit test below).

## Sources opened (Nov 14, 2026)
- **The history paths** (`sources/s74_history.py` builds `sources/s74_history.json` and a rebuild check; raw files and SHA-256 in `sources/s74_history/raw/`). From FRED: the effective federal funds rate, 10-year Treasury, 30- and 15-year mortgage rates (Freddie Mac), the Fed's total assets, its MBS and its Treasuries (year-end, as % of nominal GDP), CPI-U and core, food at home, shelter, medical care, PCE prices, unemployment and CBO's noncyclical rate, NBER recession months, real GDP and CBO potential, Case-Shiller national and FHFA home prices, S&P 500. Also the Wu-Xia shadow rate (Atlanta Fed; the file ends Feb 2022, used only in months the effective rate was below 0.25%). Shiller's real S&P Composite to Dec 2022, continued with FRED's S&P 500 over CPI-U (the overlap 2017-2022 agrees within 0.15 points a year). And the Fed's Distributional Financial Accounts (net worth by wealth group, Q4; release files of Sep 15, 2026). The 2025 monthly series hold 11 months (October 2025 was not published); the file flags them.
- **Policy-rate rule:** Taylor (1993), as written on the Federal Reserve's page "Policy Rules and How Policymakers Use Them" (opened in S72): R = r* + π + 0.5(π − 2%) + 0.5(y − y_P). Stanford's copy of the paper failed TLS from this machine; the coefficients are those of the Fed's page.
- **Purchases and long rates:** Gagnon, Raskin, Remache and Sack (2011, IJCB 7(1)). A 1%-of-GDP rise in longer-term debt held by the public raises the 10-year term premium by 4.4 basis points (6.4 in ten-year equivalents). The $1.725 trillion of purchases (12% of 2009 GDP) lowered it by 38-82 basis points (time series), and the event study gives 91 basis points. Overall range 30-100.
- **Refinancing:** Di Maggio, Kermani and Palmer (NBER w22638, 2016). GSE-eligible loans need a loan-to-value ratio at or below 80% (HARP excepted), so QE1's refinancing reached conforming borrowers with equity of 20% or more. Underwater fixed-rate borrowers were excluded.
- **Shares:** Bernanke and Kuttner (2005; FEDS 2004-16). An unexpected 25-basis-point cut goes with about a 1% rise in broad stock indexes; a −4.68% one-day return per 1 point of surprise tightening.
- **Homes:** Williams (2015, FRBSF Economic Letter, "Measuring the Effect of Monetary Policy on House Prices"). Two years after a 1-point rise in the short rate, real house prices are down by over 6% and real GDP per head by nearly 2%. Across studies, the house-price effect is 3-6 times the GDP effect (median about 4).
- **Distribution:** Lee (New York Fed Staff Report 1108, revised Oct 2026), on 2009-2015 unconventional policy. The welfare gains are "mildly U-shaped across the wealth distribution": households at the bottom gain mainly from lower unemployment, and higher profits and equity prices benefit the top. The engine reports both channels; it gives no verdict.
- **Not opened (stated, not filled in):** BLS's research CPI by income fifth (R-CPI-I; bls.gov refused this machine, HTTP 403). The check in item 4 waits for it (an amendment records it if it is opened). The forced-sale discount (Campbell, Giglio and Pathak 2011) was not opened; until it is, the discount is a design parameter (item 5).

## What is built (switches off by default; off = identical)
1. **History environment** (environment key `hx`, beside Reference, Adverse and Stress; 21 simulated years, 2005-2025). Today's population (the 2022-based start) lives through each year's actual path. This is a model counterfactual; only changes and differences are read, never levels against the data.
   - Outside inflation: CPI-U, December over December. Asset real gains: Case-Shiller national (homes and other real estate) and the S&P real price (shares and business), year-end over year-end, deflated by CPI-U. These replace ρ (vehicles and other goods keep 0).
   - Recessions, rule fixed now: the flag is on in a year with any NBER recession month, or an unemployment gap (unemployment minus CBO's rate) of 2 points or more. That gives 2008-2013 and 2020. The income multiplier in a flagged year is real median household income (`sources/history/macro_drivers.csv`) over its highest earlier level since 2005, capped at 1. The engine's existing recession job-loss rule then applies, with the income cut reduced by the extra job loss as today (MODEL_SPEC 12.1).
   - The actual rate path with the central bank off: nominal assets earn `0.46 × FEDFUNDS` (the deposit pass-through on the actual rate), and floating debt grows at `FEDFUNDS + 3.0` points (design parameter: a floating-debt spread; readings 1.5 and 4.5). Fixed debt is unchanged. Refinancing (item 2c) follows the actual 30-year rate.
   - Checked, not tuned, on the no-programme run: (i) the top 1% and bottom 50% shares of net worth against the DFA's path (direction and size of change 2005-2025, in points); (ii) basket and wealth poverty's changes against the history pack's poverty series (session 9's comparisons, extended to 2025). Verdicts use session 9's words (matches; same direction, different size; does not match). Nothing is changed after a miss; the misses are reported.
2. **A policy-rate rule in both runs** (switch `CB`, row option `cb`; needs `rt`). Each year, set from last year: `i* = r* + π + a(π − π*) + b × gap`, where π is last year's total inflation (outside plus the programme's own, the engine's price index) and gap is the output gap. Main values: a 0.5, b 0.5, π* 2%, r* 0.97% (the engine's real rate, so the rule agrees with today's savers' rate at target). The output gap is the environment's: in History, CBO's (real GDP over potential). Elsewhere it is −2 × the recession's unemployment rise, by Okun's rule of thumb (design parameter; reading −1.5). Claude's reading (modelling assumption): the programme's own change in hours is not a gap, as in S72. The policy rate is `i = max(0, i*)`.
   - a. **Purchases at the floor.** When `i* < 0`, the central bank buys long assets, sized to the shadow rate. The 10-year yield falls by `β_L × (−i*)` (β_L 0.5, design parameter; readings 0.25 and 1). The purchases implied by Gagnon et al.'s 4.4 basis points per 1% of GDP are reported each year beside the Fed's actual holdings in History (a check of size, not a target). Purchases unwind at 1/5 a year once `i* > 0` (design parameter).
   - b. **Deposits and floating debt.** Nominal assets earn `0.46 × i` and floating debt grows at `i + spread`. Today's engine reading is `i = π`: with `cb {a: 0, b: 0, r: 0}` and the spread at 0, the rule reproduces today's figures bit for bit in Reference, Adverse and Stress (unit test).
   - c. **Refinancing.** The 30-year mortgage rate moves with the 10-year yield (one for one; design parameter, reading 0.75). Each year the rate falls by 0.5 point or more below its lowest earlier level, a household whose home equity is at least 20% of its home's value (Di Maggio, Kermani and Palmer) refinances. Its fixed-rate debt falls by the present value of the payment saving: `fall × D`, with D = 7 years (design parameter: the duration of a 30-year mortgage at refinancing; readings 4 and 10), times a take-up share κ 0.5 (design parameter; readings 0.25 and 1). Households below 20% equity get nothing. No draw.
   - d. **Asset prices.** The stance is `s = i* − (r* + π)`, with the shadow rate below the floor, so purchases act here. Real share prices change by −4.68% per point of the year's change in s (Bernanke and Kuttner). Real home prices change by −3% per point in each of the two years after (Williams: about 6% after two years). These are added to the environment's real gains. In History with the rule on, only the rule's deviation from the actual stance moves prices, because the actual path already holds the actual policy.
   - e. **Unemployment.** A point of stance tightening raises unemployment by 0.5 point the next year (Williams' 2% of GDP after two years, halved by the same Okun rule as above). The job-loss chance is interpolated linearly on unemployment, between the calm rate (8.51%) and the recession rate (14.23%). The anchor is the mean unemployment of the CPS years behind `EMPL_P_REC`, read from the engine's source at the build and recorded in an amendment before the pilot. The same draw `u0` is used, so the eight-draws rule holds.
   - f. **Spending** (reading `cb.dm`, main 0): the share of the cash surplus consumed falls by `dm × s` (readings 0.5, 1; design parameter, no source opened). Main 0 because no source was opened. With the reading on, the rule can damp the programme's own demand.
   - Where S72's `gv.r 'rule'` is also on, the programme's borrowing pays this rule's `i` (plus S72's debt premium).
3. **Forward sweep** (20 seeds; session machine or Actions once runners are free; free). Both designs (the release design and C2.2, each on the round-2 main row) × four environments (Reference, Adverse, Stress, History). One setting is varied at a time from the main values: a (0.5, 1, 1.5, 2); b (0, 0.5, 1); π* (2%, 3%); r* (0, 0.97%, 1.5%); β_L (0.25, 0.5, 1); purchases off; a response lag of 0, 1 or 2 years (the rule reads π from 1, 2 or 3 years before). Plus three named regimes, fixed now: **2008-style** (a 1.5, b 1, β_L 1: fast to the floor, large purchases); **2020-style** (b 1, β_L 1, lag 0); **2022-style** (lag 2, then a 2: late and steep tightening). Results are fans: the 10th-90th percentile across seeds of each yearly path, never a single path.
4. **Who gains and who pays** (both runs; by wealth fifth and the top 1%, weighted as S59's tail). The change in real wealth is split by channel: asset prices from the rule and purchases (item 2d), deposits (2b), floating-debt cost (2b), refinancing (2c), job-loss income (2e), and forced sales (item 5). Each household's own inflation comes from its basket (shares of food, shelter, medical care and the rest; the engine's spending shares), priced at History's component paths. It is printed beside the CPI-U average, never as a hidden-inflation multiplier, and checked against R-CPI-I when that is opened.
5. **Forced sales and buying low** (reading `cb.fs`, main on with `cb`). A household that needs more than its borrowing limit (`dr.lim`) sells real assets at the year's price less a discount: 0 main, readings 0.1 and 0.27, all design parameters until Campbell, Giglio and Pathak is opened. It sells shares first, then other real estate, then its home. The buyers are households with nominal assets above six months of their spending, pro rata to those nominal assets, with no draw. Reported: real assets that change hands by group and by year, the price paid relative to the year's price, and the buyers' later gains.
6. **The stability test.** Outcomes in the next section.
7. Unit tests; a pilot at 100 seeds; a report in plain words (`dev/reports/s74-central-bank.md`).

Before the pilot: a short note, "Claude's reading of the design" (research rule 6), on what the Hub papers say about stability, essential markets and central banks. It is written after reading those papers and committed in compassionism-simulation `dev/reports/` before any pilot run. If the Hub's text names a different stability measure from those below, it is added as a secondary outcome in an amendment before the pilot, never after.

## Unit tests (written with the build)
- Off identical: guard test, and `hx` absent gives every existing environment unchanged.
- `cb {a: 0, b: 0, r: 0}` with spread 0, no purchases, no refinancing and `fs` off gives today's figures exactly in Reference, Adverse and Stress (the nesting in 2b).
- The floor: `i ≥ 0` in every year; purchases only when `i* < 0`.
- Refinancing: no household under 20% equity refinances; with κ 0, fixed debt is unchanged.
- Forced sales: real assets sold equal real assets bought each year (in quantity and at the price paid); with `fs` off, no real asset changes hands.
- Eight draws per agent-year; no non-finite output; `ACCT` identities hold with `cb` on (`r2PairCheck` with a new `R2_ALT` entry `'cb'`, three environments plus History).
- History: the built paths equal `sources/s74_history.json` (read back from the engine's copy).
- With `gv.r 'rule'` and `cb` both on, the programme's borrowing rate equals `cb`'s `i` plus the debt premium.

## Pilot (100 seeds; session machine, free)
Seeds 1-100, 500 adults, the round-2 main row (`r2MainRow`) in both runs. Reference, Adverse and Stress run 20 years; History runs 21. Both designs (release, C2.2). The rows form a 2 × 2 factorial: programme off/on × central bank off/on (`cb` main values), each paired on seeds. History also runs a third arm, the actual rate path with `cb` off.
Command (recorded in the amendment before the run): `node dev/tools/s74_pilot.js ENV 100`; tables `node dev/tools/s74_table.js`. Results: compassionism-sim-results `s74-cb-<engine commit, 7 chars>`, with `run.txt` and `SHA256SUMS`.

## Outcomes
**Primary** (the stability test; programme minus no-programme, paired on seeds, with the central bank on):
- P1. The year-to-year spread (standard deviation over years) of the change in the essentials price index (food, shelter, medical care: the basket's cost).
- P2. The spread of the year-to-year change in real disposable income, per household; the household mean, and by wealth fifth.
- P3. The largest fall in real wealth from an earlier peak (drawdown), per household; median by wealth group.
- P4. Recovery time after a recession year (the existing `rk` measures).
- P5. The share of households above the basket line the year before a recession or rate-shock year who are below it in that year.
- **The interaction** (research rule 4): the central bank's effect on P1-P5 with the programme, minus its effect without (2 × 2, paired). The claim that the PTF and PTH share of essentials reduces how far rate moves and shocks reach the cost of essentials is made only where the interaction's 95% interval excludes zero in that direction.

**Secondary:** basket poverty (`fgt0PY`) and wealth poverty (the year-20 and yearly-average `pov`), confirming any gain (guardrail 6); the policy rate, shadow rate, purchases and mortgage rate paths; the programme's own inflation (`endoAnn`) with and without the rule; the who-gains split (item 4) by group; household inflation against CPI-U; forced sales (item 5); the History checks (item 1); and S72's borrowing cost when `gv` is on.

**The plain statement required in the report:** the spread of the programme's own yearly inflation is set beside the spread of outside inflation in each environment. If the programme's own inflation is the larger source of instability, the report says so in its first paragraph.

## Analysis
- Paired differences over seeds with 95% intervals (paired t; a paired bootstrap of 2,000 replicates beside it for P3 and P5, which are skewed). Holm's correction across P1-P5 within each design and environment; the interaction is tested on the same five.
- The forward sweep (20 seeds) is descriptive: fans and ranges, with no claims of significance.
- Every cell is published, nulls and unfavourable results included (research rule 2).

## Decision rules (fixed now)
- **"The programme makes household finances steadier"** on an outcome: the Holm-adjusted interval excludes zero in the steadier direction, in that environment, for that design. "Less steady": the same, the other way. Otherwise "no measurable difference". These are said per environment. History and Adverse are read first, because they hold the shocks.
- **"The programme's share of essentials damps what the central bank and outside shocks do to the cost of basic living":** only if P1's interaction interval excludes zero in that direction in History and in at least one of Adverse or Stress, for that design, and basket poverty does not get worse.
- **"The central bank's response to the programme's inflation offsets part of the programme's gain"** (or adds to it): read from the programme-on contrast of `cb` on against `cb` off on basket and wealth poverty, with the same interval rule.
- A History check that does not match is reported as a miss in the report's limits. No constant changes because of it.

## Limits stated in advance
- Today's population lives through 2005-2025's paths; this is not a reconstruction of those years.
- The rule is a reduced form. The engine has no bond market, no banks' balance sheets and no exchange rate. The asset-price and unemployment responses are coefficients taken from the papers above, not derived in the model.
- The programme's employment effect is not fed into the gap (a cautious modelling assumption). With `cb.dm` at 0, the rule cannot damp the programme's own demand.
- The Fed's governance is described as it is in the report (Board appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate; six of each Reserve Bank's nine directors elected by member banks; since Dodd-Frank, bank-representative directors do not vote on Reserve Bank presidents). Critics are quoted with attribution, and the model's results speak for the comparison.

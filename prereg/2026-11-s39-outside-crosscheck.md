# Preregistration: outside engine cross-check of the price level (S39)

Committed Nov 8, 2026 by S39, before any run of the study below (the local checks and the one-seed pilot listed under "Checks made before writing this" came first and are disclosed there; the Actions dry run comes after this commit). Engine: BetterToBest/compassionism-simulation at the frozen commit `ce8d030b8d16a752706c4ea2db71196e11e73831` (the S47 freeze; S48's batch ran on it). Outside engine: BeforeIT.jl 0.6.0 (Bank of Italy), Julia 1.11.5, with every package fixed by the committed `Manifest.toml`. Code: compassionism-sim-results `runner/jobs/s39-path.js` (the engine side), `runner/jobs/beforeit.js` and `runner/jobs/beforeit/` (the outside side: `s39.jl` the module, `run.jl` the scenarios, `s39_shares.py` the sizes, `shares.json` the sizes used).

## Why
The Compassionism engine's price level rises fast under the programme. In study A (S48) the C2.2 design multiplies the price level by about 410 by year 20 in the Reference environment (about 37% a year), the release design by about 180, and the figures are the same at 500 and 50,000 adults. The engine's general price rule is a placeholder taken from the quantity theory of money: each year the price level rises by the share of the year's cash income that is new money not matched by new output (`lamG` = 1; "a logged placeholder, swept 0.25-1"). S39 asks an outside, independently built and published economy-wide agent-based model what it does with the same new money. BeforeIT.jl implements Poledna, Miess, Hommes and Rabitsch (2023), the first agent-based model to match standard forecasting models; it is calibrated to Austria and runs with one agent per person and per firm.

## Plain-words summary
- We give BeforeIT's Austrian economy the same amount of new money, as a share of household income year by year, as the engine's C2.2 design creates in the Reference environment, paid equally to every person, and compare its price level with its own no-payout run on the same seeds.
- The headline is how far prices rise in each engine, and BeforeIT's implied pass-through (how much of each percent of new money ends up in prices) beside the engine's 1.
- Beside it: the release design; the Adverse and Stress environments' amounts; the whole payout counted as new money (none of it matched by output); the same payout paid from taxes; a central bank that answers Austria's own inflation (as a national central bank would) instead of the euro area's; four fixed doses (1%, 3%, 10%, 30% of household income); and BeforeIT at 1:1000 beside full scale.
- What a US calibration would take is reported in words (no run).
- About 150 runner-hours on GitHub Actions (free). Every figure is published, whichever way it points.

## Checks made before writing this
1. **Engine side (no new engine figure; reading only).** `s39-path.js` reruns study A's rows (500 adults, 20 years, CPS yardstick, raise off and on, both designs, the three environments, seeds 1-500) with the engine's own `tbStudy` and copies the year path each run already returns (price levels, the unmatched money U and cash income Y). Its price level at the last year equals study A's, seed by seed, for every row in the three environments (`s39_shares.py` refuses to write the sizes otherwise). A first version left out the runner's reference settings that study A's job script sets (`applyNR6()`, `tbSetG(TB_PROFILE_G)`) and gave other price levels; it was corrected before any figure was used, and the seed-by-seed check was added. In these rows U is exactly the Source's payout less the part matched by output (`srcPay - srcM`, to the dollar per adult-year). Means over seeds 1-500, Reference, 20 years: the C2.2 Source pays $59,263 per adult-year (BU $18,289, conversion pay $40,974), $19,140 (32%) is matched by output and U is $40,123; the release design pays $31,515 with $2,727 (9%) matched. The general price level grows each year by exactly U/Y: 31% to 46% a year in C2.2 Reference (mean by year), 16% to 34% under the release design. The conversion tax the Source keeps ($12,336 per adult-year in C2.2) does not reduce U.
2. **Outside side.** BeforeIT's packaged Austria is 1:1000. Its own calibration code scales a country by multiplying the numbers of people and firms and dividing the per-agent amounts; applied to the packaged Austria at 1000 it gives 8.9 million agents (first quarters run; 40 s a quarter on 4 threads, 7.3 GB; about 100 s a quarter on one thread, 5.5 GB). With several threads BeforeIT does not repeat a seed exactly (its goods market is matched in parallel); on one thread it does (checked), so every run here is single-threaded and paired by seed.
3. **Pilot, seen before the decision rules were written (disclosed).** 1:1000, seed 1, 80 quarters: a constant payout of 10% and of 35% of household income a year, deficit-financed. The consumer price level stayed within about 8% of the no-payout run at every year shown (years 1, 5, 10, 20), real output rose (3-15%), and government debt rose without limit (to about 8 times a year's output at 35%). In the no-payout run unemployment falls to zero after year 5. The firm price rule (BeforeIT `firms.jl`) sets each price from its last value, its unit costs and expected inflation; excess demand raises output and wages (wages up to 1.5 times through the capacity term) but has no direct price term, and unmet demand stays as household deposits. These are properties of BeforeIT's design, stated here so the decision rules below do not read them as a finding.

## Questions
- **Q1 (primary).** With the engine's C2.2 Reference new money (share of household income, year by year), how much higher is BeforeIT's consumer price level than in its no-payout run, after 12 quarters and after 80 quarters? Beside it, the engine's own rise for the same row (general level and full basket level), years 3 and 20.
- **Q2 (primary).** BeforeIT's implied pass-through: λ = ln(P_payout / P_none) / Σ ln(1 + s_t) over the years to the reading (s_t the year's share). The engine's general level has λ = 1 by construction. From the matched rows and from the four fixed doses.
- **Q3.** The same for the release design and for the C2.2 amounts of the Adverse and Stress environments.
- **Q4.** Matching: the whole C2.2 payout as new money (gross) against the unmatched part only.
- **Q5.** Financing: the C2.2 payout financed by an income-tax surcharge against new money.
- **Q6.** Monetary closure: a central bank answering Austria's own inflation and growth against the euro area's (calibrated) rule.
- **Q7.** Essentials: BeforeIT's food, housing (with utilities) and medical price indices under the C2.2 payout, against the engine's essentials (full level over general level).
- **Q8.** Scale: BeforeIT at 1:1000 against full scale, as the engine's 500 against 50,000 adults.
- **Q9 (no run).** What a US calibration of BeforeIT would take.

## Design
- **Engine side.** Study A's rows rerun by `s39-path.js` on the frozen engine (above), seeds 1-500, 500 adults, 20 years (the 40-year paths are not needed: BeforeIT runs 20 years), three environments; results folder `s39-path-ce8d030` (run on the session machine, command and checksums beside it). The sizes BeforeIT receives are fixed from the 20-year rows before any BeforeIT run: for each year, the mean over seeds of U_t / Y_t (`s39_shares.py`, written to `runner/jobs/beforeit/shares.json`). Gross: the C2.2 Reference path times srcPay / (srcPay - srcM).
- **BeforeIT module** (`s39.jl`; nothing in BeforeIT's package code is edited). Each quarter the payout is the year's share times the households' total nominal income of the previous quarter (payouts included, as the engine's Y includes conversion pay), paid equally to every person through BeforeIT's per-person "other social benefits", so it counts as government spending. New money: the payout adds to the government deficit (BeforeIT keeps no separate money and bond accounts: the deficit ends as household deposits). Tax: an income-tax surcharge each quarter equal to the previous quarter's payout over the previous quarter's tax base (capped at a 95% rate). Domestic closure: BeforeIT's Taylor rule with its calibrated parameters, fed Austria's own last-quarter inflation and growth. Austria 2010Q1 calibration; quarters 1-80 (20 years).
- **Scenarios** (13; each paired seed by seed with the no-payout run of the same closure): base-ea, base-dom; c22-ref-ea, c22-ref-dom; c22-adv-ea, c22-st-ea; rls-ref-ea; c22-ref-gross-ea; c22-ref-tax-ea; dose01-ea, dose03-ea, dose10-ea, dose30-ea.
- **Environments.** The engine's three environments differ in the economy they model (recession paths, US data). They enter BeforeIT only through their new-money amounts (c22-adv, c22-st); BeforeIT's own economy is Austria's calibration throughout. Stated, not hidden.
- **Seeds and scale.** Full scale (1:1, 8.9 million agents): BeforeIT seeds 1-5 per scenario (65 runs). 1:1000: seeds 1-100 per scenario (1,300 runs). Seeds are paired across scenarios.
- **Where.** GitHub Actions, the run-study workflow, engine commit ce8d030, job `beforeit.js`. Full scale: study `s39-full`, arguments `--scale=1000 --quarters=80 --reps=5`, run indices 1-65 in 65 shards (one run per shard, about 2.5 hours each). 1:1000: study `s39-k1`, arguments `--scale=1 --quarters=80 --reps=100`, run indices 1-1300 in 13 shards. A dry run first: study `dry-s39`, `--scale=1 --quarters=4 --reps=1`, run indices 1-13 in 1 shard.

## Outcomes
- **Primary.** (a) ln(CPI_c22-ref-ea / CPI_base-ea) at quarters 12 and 80, full scale; (b) λ for the same, and for each dose at quarters 12 and 80; (c) beside them, the engine's ln(full level) and ln(general level) for C2.2 Reference at years 3 and 20 (mean over seeds 1-500), and the engine's λ (1 for the general level; the full level's own ratio).
- **Secondary.** The same for every other scenario; BeforeIT's GDP deflator, food, housing and medical indices, real GDP, unemployment, government debt over a year's output, the policy rate and the income-tax rate; at 1:1000 the same set. Every quarter of every run is published.
- Units: log points and percentages of the price level; λ unitless. The engine's poverty outcomes are not part of this study (BeforeIT has no poverty measure); none is claimed.

## Analysis
- Paired differences seed by seed. Full scale (5 seeds): mean and a t interval (95%) over seeds, with the range. 1:1000 (100 seeds): mean and a paired bootstrap interval (10,000 replicates, percentile).
- λ: the ratio of the mean log difference to the matched Σ ln(1 + s_t); its interval from the same seeds.
- No synergy is claimed (no factorial). No multiple-comparison correction: there are two primary readings, both reported.

## Decision rules (written after the pilot in check 3, before any run of the study)
1. **Agreement.** If the 95% interval of λ (c22-ref-ea, full scale, quarter 80) includes 0.5 or more, the report says the outside engine reproduces the engine's order of magnitude. If its upper end is below 0.25 (the lowest value the engine's own sweep uses), the report says the outside engine's prices rise far less than the engine's, by the measured factor. In between: partial agreement, with the figure.
2. **What it means.** A low λ is reported as what an engine whose prices do not answer excess demand directly does with the money (more output, rationed demand, deposits, imports), and a high one likewise; neither is reported as proof that the engine's figure is right or wrong. The report names the assumption each engine's figure rests on.
3. **Horizon.** Quarter 12 is inside BeforeIT's validated forecasting range; quarter 80 is not, and its no-payout run drifts (check 3). Both are reported; any sentence about 20 years carries that caveat.
4. **Consequences.** The frozen engine and its published figures do not change. If rule 1 gives "far less", S39 asks (ledger, via /end-session) that every Hub sentence on the programme's inflation say the figure rests on the engine's quantity-theory placeholder and give the outside engine's figure beside it, and the engine question (the price rule's pass-through) goes to the next engine round. The conversion tax not netted from U (check 1) goes to the same round as an open engine question.
5. **Secondary contrasts** (Q4-Q8) are reported with intervals; a difference is called a difference only where its 95% interval excludes zero.
